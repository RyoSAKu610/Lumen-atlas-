# Blossom Valley — First Fauna Roster
**Owner:** Creative Direction / ChatGPT
**Version:** 0.1 — visual roster for the first vertical slice

This roster describes original species and their readable ecological roles. Final names and measurements are provisional until the species database is normalized.

## 01 — Hanabikari (花灯狐)
- **Role:** small-to-medium social omnivore; den-forming family species.
- **Silhouette:** fox-like quadruped with a deep chest, long plume tail, broad ear fans and a soft feather mantle along shoulders.
- **Materials:** warm ivory undercoat, muted vermilion feather markings, fine mineral flecks that catch light only in dusk.
- **Movement:** quiet padded walk, sudden nimble bound, low crouch, tail counterbalance, precise paw placement on wet stone.
- **Social cues:** ear fan opens during greeting; nose touch between familiar adults; kits tumble and chase the tail plume; parent blocks the path when a threat is close.
- **Ecological function:** fruit, bulbs, insects and small prey; seed dispersal and control of small invertebrates.

## 02 — Tsukitsuno (月角鹿)
- **Role:** medium-to-large herd herbivore.
- **Silhouette:** long-legged deer-like browser with a sloping back and branching antlers that carry faint mineral growths near the tips.
- **Materials:** cool slate-blue coat with cream throat and bronze-gold antler seams.
- **Movement:** measured high-step gait, head-led turns, synchronized herd pivots, cautious water approach.
- **Social cues:** antler angle and shoulder posture communicate rank; herd members maintain spacing; juveniles mirror adult grazing.
- **Ecological function:** browsing, seed and spore transport, prey base for large predators.

## 03 — Iwabane (岩羽)
- **Role:** large omnivorous / scavenging cliff and riverbank species.
- **Silhouette:** compact bear-lizard body, shovel-like foreclaws, layered stone-textured back plates and small feather fans along the neck.
- **Materials:** moss-green scales, charcoal stone scutes, pale mineral seams.
- **Movement:** heavy deliberate steps, short climbing bursts, foreclaw digging, shoulder roll when settling.
- **Social cues:** mostly solitary; tolerates familiar individuals at abundant food; warns with a low chest rumble and raised dorsal plates.
- **Ecological function:** scavenging, digging, soil turnover and occasional fruit / shellfish feeding.

## 04 — Mizuhane (水羽)
- **Role:** semi-aquatic small social species.
- **Silhouette:** streamlined otter-axolotl hybrid with a long rudder tail, webbed forefeet and translucent fin-frills.
- **Materials:** pearl-grey skin, teal dorsal gradient, pink-gold gill frills.
- **Movement:** fluid body wave in water, tail-driven propulsion, awkward but expressive short steps on land.
- **Social cues:** chirps and surface taps; juveniles play-chase in shallow channels; grooming focuses on gill frills.
- **Ecological function:** aquatic invertebrate control and nutrient transfer between pools and river.

## 05 — Kagaribi Tsubasa (篝翼)
- **Role:** large aerial omnivore / opportunistic predator.
- **Silhouette:** long-winged feathered drake with a narrow torso, long stabilizing tail and hooked feet; avoid generic four-legged dragon anatomy.
- **Materials:** cream, rust and smoke-grey flight feathers; dark keratin beak and talons.
- **Movement:** strong takeoff push, two heavy wingbeats into a glide, wind-aware banking, talon-first landing.
- **Social cues:** aerial display flights, perch calls, pair coordination during nesting season; retreats from larger territorial flyers.
- **Ecological function:** carrion removal, small prey regulation and long-distance seed dispersal where appropriate.

## Scale and animation contrast
The first playable scene should show at least three distinct mass classes: Mizuhane (small), Hanabikari (medium), Tsukitsuno / Iwabane (large), with Kagaribi Tsubasa occupying the air volume. Do not solve scale by simply enlarging the same skeleton. Each species requires its own anatomy, gait and contact logic.
