# Blossom Valley — Environment Art Brief
**Owner:** Creative Direction / ChatGPT
**Status:** First-pass production brief
**Purpose:** First high-quality environment vertical slice

## Emotional target
The habitat should feel ancient, warm, and quietly immense. The player should feel like a wildlife observer who has entered a valley that has been alive for thousands of seasons. Beauty is not ornamental: every flowerbed, thermal pool, river shelf, cliff ledge and grove should serve a biological purpose.

## Geographic inspiration and factual boundary
Use the broad visual character of Japanese mountain valleys and geothermal spring landscapes as inspiration: steep forested slopes, a narrow river basin, mineral-rich warm water, mist, moss, cedar and deciduous layers, and seasonal snow at higher elevations. This document is a creative art brief, not a verified map of Kusatsu or any other real location. A separate Japan research workstream must validate all named real-world features before a location-specific replica is claimed.

## 16:9 establishing composition
- **Foreground:** wet dark volcanic stones, moss, delicate flowers and shallow water. Place one small species close to camera to establish scale.
- **Left midground:** an old flowering tree with a broad, contorted trunk and layered branches; a family den sits naturally among roots and stone.
- **Center:** a clear river bend and a shallow warm seep joining cooler flowing water. Steam must be subtle and localized to the geothermal inflow.
- **Right midground:** an open meadow shelf used by a herd; visible track paths connect grazing, drinking and resting zones.
- **Background:** stepped forested cliffs, distant waterfall ribbons, a high ridge with lingering snow, and atmospheric depth. Avoid a generic castle as the main focal point; any ancient structure should be a small, overgrown remnant that does not dominate the habitat.

## Ecological readability
Make the valley legible as a functioning place:
- Warm pools are small and spatially limited; they are valuable thermal resources, not giant baths.
- Cold river channels remain visually distinct from warm mineral seep zones.
- Grazing shelves show clipped grass, tracks and scat / bedding traces.
- Dense cover is available for small species and juvenile retreat.
- Elevated ledges and exposed sightlines support sentinels and aerial species.
- Narrow crossings create natural encounter points and migration bottlenecks.
- Scavenger and insect activity should be visible around fallen fruit and old remains, without clutter.

## Palette and light
- Spring: jade, soft moss, pink-white blossom, mineral blue water, warm amber sunlight.
- Summer: deeper emerald canopy, saturated river turquoise, cool shadow pockets.
- Autumn: copper fern, persimmon, muted gold grass, deep pine green.
- Winter: blue-grey snow, dark bark, steam-white highlights, small pockets of evergreen life.
- Dawn and dusk should use long atmospheric shafts; midday must retain texture in highlights. Night uses sparse bioluminescence only on species adapted to it, not a blanket neon glow.

## Camera and scale
Use a low-to-mid wildlife documentary camera height, with a 24–35mm-equivalent establishing view. Include at least one medium animal, one large herbivore, and a distant megafauna silhouette in separate depth planes. Keep eye lines, water level and vegetation height consistent with creature scale.

## Exclusions
- No people, tourists, visible resort signage, or modern buildings in the fantasy habitat.
- No interface text baked into the art.
- No excessive floating particles or uniformly glowing vegetation.
- No franchise-specific silhouettes or recognizable copied landmarks.

## Engine handoff
Deliver a layered master environment with separate terrain, vegetation clusters, water, geothermal steam, distant atmosphere and optional ruins. Supply a clean lighting setup for dawn / noon / dusk / night and clear / rain / fog / snow variants. The environment artist should document world scale, water surfaces, collision, navigation affordances and performance LODs.
