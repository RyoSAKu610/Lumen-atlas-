# Creature Body Language & Interaction Visual Direction
**Owner:** Creative Direction / ChatGPT
**Audience:** Animation TD, Creature AI, Gameplay Engineering

## Core rule
Every interaction must be legible before, during and after contact. The animation should communicate intent, consent / refusal, confidence, fear, rank, affection or escalation through the body.

## Shared interaction grammar
1. **Notice:** eyes, ears, head or sensory organs orient toward the other individual.
2. **Assess:** body weight shifts; the animal pauses, lowers or raises its posture.
3. **Signal:** tail, crest, feathers, antlers, vocalization or scent gesture communicates intent.
4. **Response:** the other individual answers, ignores, retreats or approaches.
5. **Action:** only compatible partners move into synchronized contact.
6. **Outcome:** greeting, play, grooming, submission, separation or conflict changes relationship state.
7. **Recovery:** breathing, posture and spacing return to a natural state; the event becomes memory.

## Interaction direction

### Familiar greeting
Approach on a shallow arc, reduce speed before contact, turn head toward the partner, exchange a short nose / cheek touch, then separate by a natural half-step. Familiarity increases duration; strangers use a longer assessment phase.

### Play
Use invitation signals before the chase: lowered forequarters, loose tail, sideways hop, quick retreat. The partner must reciprocate. Juveniles make more exaggerated, imperfect movements; adults use controlled restraint. Play ends when fatigue, fear or resource conflict interrupts it.

### Grooming
One animal offers a body region; the partner moves in with deliberate small head motions. The recipient can lean in, reposition, or stop the interaction. Grooming affects relationship state and parasite / comfort state only if the species profile supports it.

### Warning and dominance
Do not jump directly to a fight. Use distance, eye line, body height, crest / antler display, ground scrape, low vocalization and approach-retreat feints. Many encounters should resolve without contact.

### Fighting
Show anticipation and credible body mechanics: planted feet, weight transfer, feint, dodge, lunge, contact, separation and recovery. Every move needs a contact point and a valid body-mass response. Avoid repetitive wrestling loops. Injury, fatigue, fear and dominance memory alter later decisions.

### Parent and juvenile
Parents should monitor the juvenile while doing other tasks. Young individuals seek proximity when startled, follow imperfectly, play, tire quickly and learn by imitation. Parent protection can interrupt feeding, rest and social activity.

## Technical animation contract
For each paired clip, document:
- Participant A / B roles and required anatomy family.
- Entry distance, facing and acceptable angle.
- Root motion and sync markers.
- Contact socket / surface and tolerance.
- Reaction clip / additive pose for the partner.
- Interrupt conditions (threat, pain, fatigue, target loss).
- Exit distance and cooldown.
- In-engine capture showing both participants.

## Quality gate
If two creatures play the same animation regardless of relationship, body size, fear, age or context, the behavior system is not complete.
