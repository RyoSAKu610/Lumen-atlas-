# Art Direction & World Bible

## Creative north star
LIVING ATLAS should feel like discovering a real wildlife documentary in an impossible world: awe, tenderness, scale, curiosity, tension, and quiet everyday life. The ecosystem should feel older than the observer and continue without being staged for the camera.

## Visual language
- Original creature designs and original world lore; do not reproduce recognizable franchise designs.
- Painterly environmental design informed by hand-painted 1990s Japanese OVA backgrounds, paired with modern cinematic lighting, rich material detail, and believable scale.
- 16:9 cinematic compositions with strong foreground / midground / distant scale separation.
- Rich but controlled palettes: spring jade and sakura pink; deep-sea indigo and bioluminescent cyan; canopy greens and amber shafts; volcanic ember reds and mineral blacks; highland sky blues and aurora violet.
- No human characters as a default. The creatures and their habitats are the protagonists.
- Interface should be quiet, legible, elegant, and documentary-like. Do not bake UI text into environment art.

## Creature design principles
- Each species needs a recognizable silhouette, anatomy, locomotion, ecological niche, diet, sensory strengths, social pattern, reproduction strategy, and environmental tolerance.
- Distinguish species through anatomy and movement, not just palette swaps.
- Establish size hierarchy: tiny symbionts, small scavengers, medium social species, large herbivores, apex predators, and megafauna.
- Anatomy should inform animation: body mass, gait, stride, center of gravity, tail balance, wing loading, buoyancy, and foot contact.

## Emotional range
The world should support peaceful, playful, intimate, curious, intimidating, dangerous, protective, and tragic moments. Avoid making all creatures cute or all encounters violent. Calm moments are as important as spectacle.

## Japan x fantasy
Build recognizable real-world Japanese landscapes from research, then add fictional ecology carefully:
- Kusatsu Onsen: Yubatake and mountain geothermal context.
- Noboribetsu: Jigokudani volcanic valley and geothermal terrain.
- Beppu: Kannawa steamscape and varied hot-spring topography.
- Hakone: Owakudani, Lake Ashi, caldera and mountain forest context.
- Yufuin: Mount Yufu, basin, mist, grassland and forest context.

Real landmarks, terrain, climate, and cultural details must be researched and identified as factual references. Creature habitats, magical geology, and fictional ruins must be labeled as creative additions.

## Art deliverable checklist
- Environment concept: wide establishing shot, human-free, clear habitat identity.
- Creature sheet: front / side / rear, scale reference, silhouette, material callouts.
- Behavior sheet: clear start, anticipation, contact/action, follow-through, recovery.
- Lighting variants: dawn, noon, dusk, night; weather where relevant.
- Engine-ready exports: documented dimensions, format, color space, alpha convention, naming and license / provenance.
